WHAT FUJÍTIAN ACTUALLY DOES

Word order, inflection, and possession

Fujítian is a Romance-rooted constructed language with an unusually regular grammatical system, developed over years from early vocabulary lists. Many words have Latin-derived shapes, but their relationships follow distinctly Fujítian rules.

Take “Diego gives Anna his food.” The revised guide places the recipient Anna immediately after the subject, then the possessed direct object cóma’so, and finally the verb ofra. The suffix ’so links the food to the acting subject:

Diego Anna cóma’so ofra.Diego gives Anna his food.Grammar reference · Objects, pp. 6–8

04 / GRAMMAR REFERENCE

Fujítian grammar reference

The important feature of Fujítian is not simply that verbs are conjugated or adjectives are post-nominal. It is how those patterns interact: clause structure controls word order, the shape of a verb carries agreement, and attached markers identify exactly whose object belongs to whom.

HOW TO USE THIS REFERENCE

Choose a topic below. Examples in Fujítian are taken from the supplied grammar reference or from the Diego and Daniel passages. Inference and unresolved details are explicitly marked.

CLAUSE WORD ORDER

Subject → complements → verb

Where English usually puts the finite verb immediately after the subject, Fujítian normally holds the action until after its objects or other complements. Determiners precede nouns, but adjectives follow them. The grammar guide permits omitting a subject pronoun when conjugation already makes the actor clear.

Finite verb after the complement

In the Daniel passage, the walking action is expressed as an infinitive and appears before the decision verb. This is a more revealing example than a short subject–object–verb sentence.

Dániel mópedèt ða domem élegèta.Daniel decided to take a walk through town.Daniel passage · sentence 1

Adjectives follow their nouns

Fujítian preserves the conventional order of descriptions among adjectives but places them after the noun. So the sequence “small white dog” becomes a noun followed by size and color.

canin min plúselumismall white dogGrammar reference · Word Order, p. 11

Relative clauses follow their noun phrases

Unlike a final aside, a Fujítian relative clause sits immediately after its noun phrase. The Daniel story places qeho (“whom”) directly after the old friend, then places the negative perfect inside the relative clause.

ami ança qeho no sel’è occutada in annoman old friend whom he hadn't seen in yearsDaniel passage · sentence 3
Precision note: The revised worked example places the named recipient before the direct object and finite verb: Diego Anna cóma’so ofra. Its pronoun version attaches recipient and direct object before the verb: Sol sal’a’ofra.
INTERACTIVE FINITE VERB SYSTEM

Verb endings by tense and person

For regular verbs, the infinitive ends in -t. The last two characters form an infinitive ending that is replaced or extended to encode agreement, tense, and aspect. A past form retains the infinitive's t; future adds ī; continuous adds ē, with a special first-person singular present continuous form.

PERFECT CONSTRUCTIONS

The auxiliary è and verbal adjectives

Perfect meanings do not use the ordinary finite endings alone. The construction includes a pronoun attached to è and a verbal adjective in -ada. The participial form itself distinguishes present, past, or future time; continuous perfect adds -ē.

The infinitive serves as the gerund form according to the reference. Separately, a present participle replaces the final t with u; a past participle adds -u after the past form, including its tense ending.

ARTICLES & ELISION

Article attachment and elision

e corresponds to “a/an”; le to “the.” Apostrophes bind articles to nouns. Before vowel-initial nouns, the article can elide or alter its vowel rather than remain a standalone word.

consonant-initial noune’tomon / le’tomona man / the man
vowel-initial noun’anno / l’annoa year / the year
vowel-initial noun’equìn / l’equìna horse / the horse

Demonstratives: lit / lim and lat / lam

Demonstratives combine with the ordinary noun phrase; the guide's long clause begins Lat canin min plúselumi (“that small white dog”).

Possessor after possessed noun

In English, “Cameron's house” puts the owner before the noun. Fujítian writes the possessed noun first and connects the owner with o.

domin o’CámeronCameron's houseGrammar reference · Possessives, p. 7
RELATIVE AND QUESTION WORDS

The u distinguishes asking from linking.

In a relative clause, the question-family word begins with qe-. To ask a direct question, an u is inserted after its opening q. This contrasts qeloc (“where” in a relative clause) with queloc (“where?”).

The dictionary also records derived nominal words, such as qeqisloc (“wherever”), by inserting -qis- after the relative prefix.

QUESTION AND RELATIVE FORMS

Relative versus interrogative wording

relative · what I wantLat es qema desri.That is what I want.
interrogative · when?¿Quechro sómniete?When did you go to bed?
interrogative · which?¿Quenum élegète?Which did you pick?

Direct questions carry opening and closing question marks. Relative expressions are embedded within other clauses.

Relative clauses in the Daniel passage

The Daniel sentence places the description of the friend immediately after ami ança. The clause includes qeho and the negative perfect no sel’è occutada.

Position of no in negated clauses

The guide places no at the beginning of the clause it negates. In nested constructions, that can mean the negative particle occurs within the larger sentence rather than at its very beginning.

ADJECTIVE POSITION

Adjective order

Adjectives go after the nouns they modify. Within that post-nominal group, the guide preserves a fixed descriptive order: opinion, size, age, shape, color, origin, material, purpose (after any determiner or quantity).

canin min plúselumismall white dogGrammar reference · Word order, p. 11
contena min de’timbussmall wooden boxDiego story · sentence 3

Adjectives are pluralized by changing their ending according to the guide's adjective agreement rule.

ADVERBS AND COMPARATIVES

Adjective → manner, degree, quality

A base adjective can produce an adverb (via a -b- suffix), a comparative (-pl-), and an abstract noun (-s or -us). Accent placement retains the appropriate word stress.

strong · adjectivefortisfórtisbi = strongly · fort’plis = stronger · fórtisus = strength
quick · adjectiveveliçivelíçibi = quickly · veliçpli = quicker · veliçis = speed

Adverb position after the verb

English “quickly went” becomes a motion verb followed by its derived adverb. The Daniel translation puts velíçibi after mólèta.

mólèta velíçibiwent quicklyDaniel passage · sentence 4

Adjunct nouns

The guide describes noun modifiers built from a noun and a connective: de’ before a consonant and d’ before a vowel. The Daniel translation describes a wooden box with de’timbus—a noun-based material qualifier rather than a simple adjective.

e’contena min de’timbusa small wooden boxDiego story · sentence 3; grammar · p. 11
IMPERATIVES

Commands have a special ja’ prefix.

The verb is conjugated in the second-person present; ja’ forms the imperative. A direct-object pronoun can appear before ja’, while an indirect-object recipient is attached after the verb.

commandja’ofregive!
command + direct objecta’ja’ofregive it!
command + direct & indirect objecta’ja’ófre’jaīgive it to me!

The grammar illustrates opening exclamation marks for exclamatory imperatives and describes an additional mark for a non-exclamatory imperative.

SOUND & WRITING

Apostrophe functions

The source uses apostrophes in several positions: between attached grammatical elements, when neighboring identical sounds contract, in articles and possessive phrases, and to separate certain consonant clusters. These are related spelling conventions but not all the same grammatical operation.

same-letter contractionle di’ássařotiduring the day I helped

PROSODY

Stress and accent marks

The default stress falls on the penultimate syllable. Acute accents mark exceptional stress and may preserve original stress after suffixation and attachment; the source gives vivís and dósidia as examples.

All ordinary vowels are independently pronounced, with ai, au, oa among the documented one-syllable combinations. The writing system distinguishes a single-tap r from a trilled ř, and sounds such as ç /s/ and ș /ʃ/.

REFERENCE NOTES

Reference sources and exceptions

Fujítian is under active revision. The grammar guide contains a few typographical inconsistencies, competing statements, and forms that are not fully explained. The reference preserves documented example sentences and maintains documented translations without changes, rather than extrapolating an unverified rule or silently changing a spelling. A fixed example does not prove every similar form has exactly the same behavior.

Sources: Fujítian Language (13-page manuscript); Fujitian Translation Dictionary (22-page lexical sheet); the Diego and Daniel translations supplied by the reference. Original script glyphs are not invented on this website.

Tapped r and trilled ř

r is a single tongue tap /ɾ/, like Spanish pero; ř is a rolling trill /r/, like perro. The distinction changes how a word sounds.

Vowel combinations

Most vowels are pronounced separately, but ai, au, oa are treated as one-syllable combinations unless an accent marks a separation. The guide also distinguishes i / ē from ì and ī.

08 / DEVELOPMENT AND SOURCES

Development of Fujítian

Fujítian began as vocabulary lists in childhood and was developed into a formal grammar during high school. Its rules are designed to be consistent and mostly regular visible in every sentence.

HISTORY OF THE LANGUAGE

Vocabulary and grammar development

According to the manuscript's foreword, the project began around age three, accumulated as word lists, and was later assembled into a dictionary and a grammar. Latin-based vocabulary, some Spanish-like conjugational ideas, and some English features were adapted into a highly regular structure.

Minimizing irregularity is a central design goal. The grammar identifies vit (“to be”) as the one irregular verb, while the language remains open to revision.

SOURCES AND LIMITATIONS

Example sources

The interactive grammar and dictionary draw on the reference's own source files. Story and lab text reproduce the supplied English and Fujítian passages rather than generating unreviewed sentences.
