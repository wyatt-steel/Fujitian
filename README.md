# Fujítian

**Fujítian is a constructed Romance language with a predominantly Latin-derived vocabulary, highly regular grammar, and a distinctive system of word order, conjugation, and orthography.**

Development of Fujítian began as an early childhood language project and gradually evolved into a structured language with its own vocabulary, pronunciation rules, grammatical system, and original writing script.

Although Fujítian draws much of its vocabulary from Latin and incorporates elements reminiscent of Spanish and English, its grammar follows an independently developed set of rules. A central design principle is grammatical consistency, with predictable verb endings, systematic word formation, and very few irregularities.

## Linguistic Features

Fujítian differs from English and other Romance languages in several important ways:

- **Subject–Complement–Verb word order:** Objects and other complements generally precede the main verb, while indirect objects follow distinct placement rules.
- **Post-nominal adjectives and adverbs:** Descriptive words generally follow the nouns or verbs they modify.
- **Regular verb conjugation:** Verbs conjugate according to person, number, tense, and aspect, including simple, continuous, perfect, and conditional constructions.
- **Object pronoun incorporation:** Direct and indirect object pronouns can combine with conjugated verbs through apostrophe-linked constructions.
- **Possessive relationships:** Possessive forms can distinguish whether the possessor is the subject, direct object, indirect object, or an independently named person.
- **Systematic word formation:** Plurals, comparatives, adverbs, participles, and other grammatical forms follow established morphological rules.
- **Distinctive phonology and orthography:** Fujítian uses modified Latin characters, diacritics, predictable stress patterns, and phonemic distinctions such as `e` /e/ versus `è` /ɛ/, and tapped `r` /ɾ/ versus trilled `ř` /r/.

### Example Translation

**English:**
> Diego gives Anna his food.

**Fujítian:**
> Diego Anna cóma’so ofra.

The translation demonstrates how Fujítian rearranges sentence elements and incorporates possession into the noun phrase.

## About This Repository

This repository contains an interactive website for learning and exploring Fujítian.

### Features

- **Grammar Reference** — Explanations and examples of sentence structure, verb conjugation, pronouns, possession, and other grammatical rules.
- **Dictionary** — A searchable English–Fujítian dictionary containing over 1,000 entries.
- **Pronunciation Guide** — Interactive IPA vowel and consonant charts with audio examples.
- **Verb Conjugator** — Generate conjugated forms of Fujítian verbs across different tenses, aspects, persons, and numbers.
- **Bilingual Passages** — Read English and Fujítian texts side by side, with interactive word alignment and grammatical explanations.
- **Sentence Breakdown** — Explore a complex sentence through color-coded grammatical analysis and translation steps.
- **Practice Exercises** — Interactive vocabulary and grammar questions with feedback.
- **Beta Translator** — Experimental, rule-based English–Fujítian translation supporting selected grammatical structures and marking unknown vocabulary.

## Technology

The website is built using:

- HTML
- CSS
- JavaScript

All features run locally in a web browser without requiring a server or external libraries. Pronunciation audio is embedded directly into the website.

## Limitations

The English–Fujítian translator is currently in beta. It uses dictionary entries and grammatical rules rather than a trained language model.

Complex sentence structures, ambiguous vocabulary, and certain grammatical constructions may not translate correctly. Unsupported vocabulary is displayed in square brackets.

## Development

Fujítian remains an evolving language. Its vocabulary, grammar, orthography, and associated learning tools continue to be expanded and refined.

The objective is to develop a coherent, expressive, and learnable language built around systematic grammatical principles.
